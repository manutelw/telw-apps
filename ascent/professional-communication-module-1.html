Module 1

Giving a Manager an Update

Learn to handle a short manager conversation: answer the first question, listen to the follow-up, and respond naturally and professionally.

1 · Train
2 · Practise
3 · Test
4 · Review

Train

A good update is a conversation, not a speech. Listen for status, risk, action, next step and natural spoken English.

Show me

A useful update answers four questions

  • Where are we now?
  • What is the main issue or risk?
  • What have you done about it?
  • What happens next — or what do you need?

Keep the relationship right

  • Polite and respectful
  • Direct, but not blunt
  • Natural, but not matey
  • Calm ownership without over-explaining

Weak exchange

Manager: “Ravi, can you give me a quick update on the report, please? Are we still on track?”
Employee: “Well, there’s been a bit of a problem with the report. I was waiting for some figures from the other team and, yeah, I’m following up with them. So hopefully I should have it done soon.”
Manager: “Right. When do you expect to have it?”
Employee: “I’m not completely sure yet. I’m waiting to hear back from them.”

Stronger exchange

Manager: “Ravi, can you give me a quick update on the report, please? Are we still on track?”
Employee: “Sure. The report’s running about a day behind because the final data came in late. I’ve finished the sections I could, and I’ve followed up for the missing figures. At this rate, I should have the full report across by tomorrow afternoon.”
Manager: “Right. What’s the big risk now, and do you need anything from me?”
Employee: “Well I’d say the only real risk is the missing figures. I’ve already followed up, so I’ll give that a little more time. If they still haven’t come through, I’ll let you know straight away and we can decide the best way forward.”

Notice: “I’ve finished the sections I could” fits the time meaning here. The model uses spoken English, not a written answer read aloud.

Voice focus · London English

Notice the stress change in update: an UP-date (noun), but to up-DATE (verb). Keep it natural — no exaggerated RP.

Practise

The manager will ask two questions. Answer each one aloud. You may retry the whole exchange after feedback.
