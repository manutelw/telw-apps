What goes into the portfolio

The portfolio follows a fixed nine-screen logic so it stays short, evidence-led and interview-friendly.

1

Start with identity and direction

Open with your name, target direction and a clear professional summary. Avoid a life story. The first two screens should tell the viewer who you are and what you bring.

Screens 3–5 · Your proof stories
Hero storyYour strongest piece of evidence.
Experience 2Another useful project or internship example.
Experience 3A third proof point that adds range.
2

Show work, not adjectives

Instead of saying “hardworking” or “creative”, show what you worked on, what you did and what changed. Screens 3–5 make the portfolio credible.

3

Turn skills into visible evidence

Use Screen 6 for a visual skills infographic and Screen 7 for role fit: what the role needs and how you can contribute. This is where the portfolio becomes more than a prettier CV.

Screens 8–9 · Achievements + Close
Achievements

4

Finish with proof and a clear close

End with selected achievements and a concise closing screen. The viewer should finish knowing your direction, your strongest evidence and how to contact you.

Why it is useful

A CV tells. An E-Portfolio can show.

The value is not decoration. It is the ability to make evidence easier to see and easier to discuss in an interview.

ShortExactly nine screens keeps the story focused.
VisualProjects, proof and contribution are easier to scan.
Interview-readyEvery screen can become a talking point in a conversation with a recruiter.
