Module 11

Escalate a Problem Without Passing It Upward

Escalate when the problem needs authority, access or a decision you do not have — but take ownership of everything you can do first.

1 · Train
2 · Practise
3 · Test
4 · Review

Train

Escalation is not handing the problem to your manager. Show what is happening, what you have already done, why it now needs their involvement, and what decision or support you need.

Show me

The escalation framework

Use this when you have reached the limit of your authority, access or influence and the issue now needs your manager’s involvement.

1. FlagWhat is the issue, and why does it matter now?
2. ActWhat have I already done at my level?
3. LimitWhat cannot I resolve without more authority, access or support?
4. AskWhat exact decision, action or support do I need?
Flag → Act → Limit → Ask

What to notice

  • Start with the issue and its current business impact or risk.
  • State what you have already tried or completed before escalating.
  • Be clear about the boundary: what needs authority, access or cross-team intervention you do not have.
  • Ask for one specific decision or action from the manager.
  • Keep ownership of the next step after the manager acts.

Keep the relationship right

  • Do not dramatise the problem or make the manager discover the facts through questions.
  • Do not escalate work you could reasonably solve yourself.
  • If the manager gives a decision, confirm what you will do next.
  • Keep the escalation short enough for the manager to act quickly.

Weak exchange

Manager: “The client approval has been waiting with another team for two days. What is the position now?”
Employee: “I’m still waiting for them. I’ve sent a few messages, but they have not replied, so there is not much I can do.”
Manager: “What exactly do you need from me?”
Employee: “Could you please handle it?”

Why this is weak: the learner passes the problem upward without showing the business impact, the actions already taken, the exact blocker or the specific help required.

Stronger exchange

Manager: “The client approval has been waiting with another team for two days. What is the position now?”
Employee: “The client approval is now two days late, and it will hold up tomorrow’s release. I’ve followed up twice and sent the missing information they asked for. The blocker is that only their team lead can approve it. Could you contact the team lead today or authorise me to escalate it to them directly? Once we have that approval, I can finish the release check immediately.”
Manager: “If I contact the team lead, what will you do next?”
Employee: “I’ll send them the approval pack again with the release deadline, copy you for visibility, and follow up after an hour. If they approve it, I’ll complete the release check straight away.”
Flag“The client approval is now two days late, and it will hold up tomorrow’s release.”
Act“I’ve followed up twice and sent the missing information they asked for.”
Limit“The blocker is that only their team lead can approve it.”
Ask“Could you contact the team lead today or authorise me to escalate it to them directly?”

Notice the shift: the stronger response does not dump the problem upward. It shows ownership first and asks the manager only for the part that genuinely needs their authority.

What changed?

Weak escalation

  • Passes the problem upward
  • Does not show prior action
  • Blocker is unclear
  • Request is vague

Professional escalation

  • Flags the impact
  • Shows ownership
  • Names the authority limit
  • Asks for specific help

Good escalation means solving everything you can first, then asking for the smallest piece of help that genuinely requires more authority.

Practise

Your manager will ask about a blocked task. Escalate only the part that needs their authority, then handle one follow-up fact.
