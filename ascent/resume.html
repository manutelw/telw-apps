What a stronger résumé does

It helps the reader see what you did, what changed and why the evidence matters for the role.

1

Start with positioning

Your summary should quickly establish your current status, the strengths you can prove, and the direction you are seeking. Avoid generic labels that anybody could claim.

Profile summary
Current status
Strength + proof
Target direction
Experience bullet · RAC logic
ResultWhat changed?
ActionWhat did you do?
ContextWhere / why?
ProofCan you defend it?
2

Replace duties with evidence

Strong bullets show impact and action. Where a measurable result exists, bring it forward. Where it does not, make the contribution and context concrete enough to discuss.

3

Check relevance to the target role

Not every true fact belongs equally high on the page. Give more space and visibility to evidence that supports the job you are actually applying for.

Role relevance check
HighDirectly supports the JD.
MediumUseful transferable evidence.
LowTrue, but weakly connected.
Interview defence
Recruiter: “You say you improved engagement. What exactly did you change, and how do you know it worked?”
Every strong claim can attract a follow-up
4

Prepare to defend the final document

A polished line is only useful if you can explain it. The résumé should become a bank of evidence you can speak about naturally when the interviewer probes.

Why it matters

The résumé is not the interview. It sets up the interview.

Better positioning helps you earn attention; stronger evidence helps you survive the questions that follow.

ClarityThe reader understands your profile quickly.
EvidenceClaims are supported by actions and results.
DefenceYou know what proof sits behind every important line.
