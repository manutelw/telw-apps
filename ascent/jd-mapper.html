2

The questions available

Your interview set always covers Introduction, Behavioural, Industry, Situational and Curveball questions. When the JD calls for them, JD Mapper can also add Role/Technical, Values & Culture, Commercial Judgement and Data/Tools questions.

3

How to practise the questions

After you review the map, select Build My Questions. Then choose Create my speaking practice. ASCENT moves the JD-specific questions into its speaking-practice flow so you can record, review and improve your spoken answers instead of merely reading a list.

A simple response framework
T · TellGive the direct answer.
E · EngageExplain with proof or an example.
L · LinkConnect the proof to the role or company.
W · WrapClose with your judgement or direction.
Example: “This role fits me because I have already handled customer-facing work. During my internship, I managed follow-ups and resolved routine queries. That experience is relevant here because the JD stresses client coordination. I would now like to build that strength in a full-time role.”
4

Frameworks to follow

The Mapper gives you the questions; your response still needs structure. A useful base is TELW: Tell → Engage/Explain → Link → Wrap. Keep the answer direct, use real evidence, and connect it back to the JD rather than giving a generic memorised response.

5

Why being JD-specific matters

Generic interview practice asks what any interviewer might ask. JD Mapper starts from this employer’s role and your evidence for it. That helps you spend preparation time on the requirements most likely to matter—and on the gaps you may actually have to defend.

What makes it different

It connects the JD, your CV and your speaking practice.

The distinctive value is not simply generating interview questions. It is the chain from role requirement → evidence fit → question → spoken practice.
