How a PCL module works

Each module teaches one practical workplace communication capability, then makes the learner use it.

1

Learn a usable workplace structure

In the manager-update module, the learner is taught to cover the current position, the reason or risk, action already taken, and the next step or support needed.

The employee’s update framework
Current positionWhere are we now?
Reason / riskWhat is causing the issue?
Action takenWhat have I done?
Next stepWhat happens now?
Hear the difference
Weak: vague status, too much background, no clear next step.
Stronger: clear position → reason → action → next step.
2

Compare weak and stronger dialogue

PCL does not only tell the learner what “good communication” means. It shows how a weak exchange sounds, then contrasts it with a stronger professional response.

3

Practise as a conversation

The learner answers a manager’s first question, listens to the follow-up and responds to the new question instead of repeating the whole speech. That is closer to real workplace communication.

4

Use feedback to improve the next attempt

The module combines content and spoken delivery: clarity, relationship, phrasing, pace, emphasis and responsiveness. The learner can compare attempts rather than simply finish a lesson.

Why it is distinct

PCL trains communication behaviour, not memorised scripts.

The focus is on how workplace conversations move and how the learner thinks and responds inside them.
