Understand the structure

Learn what each part does before you try it.

Hook

A hook gets the audience’s attention and makes them want to continue listening. It could be a surprising fact, a thoughtful question, a short story, a serious problem, an unexpected comparison or a clear challenge.

Topic: reducing employee turnover
“One out of every four new employees in our company leaves during the first year.”

Relevance

Relevance tells the audience why the subject matters to them. State it near the beginning. Do not make the audience wait to discover why they should care.

“Replacing these employees is costing the company money, delaying projects and placing additional pressure on experienced staff.”

Credibility

Credibility gives the audience a reason to trust you. Mention only the experience, investigation or evidence connected to this presentation.

“Over the past six weeks, I have reviewed our exit interviews and spoken to managers in all four departments.”

Subject

The subject tells the audience exactly what the presentation will cover. Make it specific enough for the audience to know where you are taking them.

“Today, I will present three practical changes that could reduce first-year employee turnover.”
