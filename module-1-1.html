1. Learn the language first

Read the rules. Use Listen only when you want warm voice support.

Language + thinking

Present Perfect Simple

Form: have / has + past participle.

Use it for experience before now when the exact finished time is not important.

I’ve worked on a group project before.
She has handled client queries.
We’ve completed several presentations.
PAST NOW |------ X ------ X ----------| experience happened ↓ relevant now

What does “relevant now” mean? The experience happened in the past, but it tells the listener something useful about you now: what you can do, what you have handled before, or what experience you bring to the present situation.

Example: I’ve handled client complaints before.
Why is this relevant now? The listener may be deciding whether you can handle a similar situation today.

Present Perfect → Simple Past

Use the Present Perfect to introduce experience. Once you move to one specific finished experience, use the Simple Past for the details.

Experience: I’ve worked on several team projects.
Finished detail: Last semester, I coordinated a four-member team.
PRESENT PERFECT: experience up to NOW PAST -------------------------- NOW SIMPLE PAST: one finished event PAST X NOW last semester

Important rule: Do not use the Present Perfect with a finished past time. Words and phrases such as yesterday, last week, last summer, in 2024 tell us when the finished event happened. Use the Simple Past.

✗ We have been to the college last summer.
✓ We went to the college last summer.
✗ I have completed the project last week.
✓ I completed the project last week.
✗ She has joined the company in 2024.
✓ She joined the company in 2024.
✗ We have presented the report yesterday.
✓ We presented the report yesterday.

Compare: I’ve completed several projects. But: I completed one of them last week.

BEFORE

Use before to reassure the listener that the situation is not new to you.

I’ve handled this kind of situation before.
I’ve presented in front of a class before.
PAST EXPERIENCE ✓ -------- NOW familiarity now

Managerial thinking to notice

WHAT?What experience?
WHY?Why did it matter?
PROOF?What action, fact or metric?
SO WHAT?What was the impact?
WHAT NEXT?What did you learn or change?

TELW remains the response structure. These questions are the managerial thinking inside it.

Read + listen: a professional experience answer

Manager: Have you worked on a team project where you had to manage a tight deadline?
Learner: Yes, I have. I’ve worked on several group projects during my MBA. One of the more demanding ones was a market-research project last semester.

Our team had four members, and we had only five days to complete the final report. I took responsibility for coordinating the work because two sections were still incomplete.

I divided the remaining tasks, checked the progress each evening, and helped combine the final report. We submitted it on time.

The experience taught me that clear responsibilities and short progress checks can reduce last-minute problems. Since then, I try to make roles and deadlines clear at the beginning of a team task.

See the thinking inside the answer

WHAT?I’ve worked on several group projects during my MBA.

WHY?We had only five days to complete the final report.

PROOF?Our team had four members; I divided the remaining tasks and checked progress each evening.

SO WHAT?We submitted it on time.

WHAT NEXT?Since then, I try to make roles and deadlines clear at the beginning.

Language: Present Perfect introduces experience; Simple Past gives finished details. Thinking: claim → reason → evidence → impact → learning.
