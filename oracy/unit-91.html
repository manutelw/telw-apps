Background

Learn the language first, hear it in context, practise it with support, and then speak independently.

Learning path: Warm-up → meaningful listening → notice the language → controlled speaking → supported answer → feedback and correction → harder speaking → independent performance.

Recording privacy: your recording is used only to generate feedback. ORACY does not save it.

Key Point 1 · Talking about events in life

Build a clear life line

grow upleave schoolgo to collegejoin a companyget promoted
1 · Warm-up · Speak first

Where did you grow up? What did you do after school? Where are you now?

Answer in two or three sentences. Do not worry about mistakes yet.

2 · Before you listen

Shazia is telling Simran about her background. Listen for the order of the events: school → college → travel → first job → Delhi.

3 · Listen · Shazia talks to Simran

Simran: Shazia, did you grow up in Delhi?

Shazia: No, I grew up in Quetta. When I left school, I went to college in Lahore and studied chemistry for three years.

Simran: What did you do after you graduated?

Shazia: I took a year off and travelled. I saw quite a lot of Asia and Europe, and I also visited some places in South America.

Simran: That must have been a big experience.

Shazia: It was. It really opened my eyes. I met some interesting people and travelled as cheaply as I could, mostly by bus, train and low-cost flights.

Simran: And what happened when you came back?

Shazia: I needed to find my first job. My qualifications and the confidence I had gained helped me make a good impression, and I got a position at a centre in Delhi.

Simran: Had you always planned to move away from Pakistan?

Shazia: No. Before travelling, I had never really thought about it. But after I came back, the world seemed much smaller.

Simran: And how do you feel about Delhi now?

Shazia: I recently bought a house outside Delhi, and now I feel at home here.

4 · Notice the language in the conversation

Do not learn single words. Learn the event as a chunk:

grow up → leave school → go to college → graduate → take a year off → travel → come back → find a job → get a position → move to a city

Notice how one event leads to the next. That is what makes a background easy to follow.

Key Point 2 · Discussing past events

Tell a past story clearly

past simpleregular verbsirregular verbssequence

1 · Warm-up

Think about yesterday or last weekend. What happened first? What happened next?

2 · Listen · A short past story

Nikhil: How was your weekend?

Meera: Busy. I visited my cousin on Saturday and helped her move house.

Nikhil: Did anything go wrong?

Meera: We lost one key, but we found it under a box later. Then we went out for lunch.

Nikhil: So it ended well.

Meera: Yes. By evening we were exhausted, but everything was done.

3 · Notice the language

Finished past events use the past simple. Regular verbs often end in -ed. Common irregular forms include went, saw, met, took, found, bought, made, came. Use first, then, after that, later, finally to keep the story easy to follow.

4 · Controlled task

Say the past form aloud: go, meet, take, find, buy, make, come. Then use any two in one short sentence.

Rep 1 · Easy supported attempt

Say three things you did yesterday.

5 · Correction / clarification

Check one verb form from your feedback. Correct it, then repeat the sentence once.

Rep 2 · Sequence

Describe a short event using first, then and finally.

Rep 3 · Trip

Talk about a trip using went, saw and met.

Rep 4 · Problem and result

Describe a small problem and how it ended.

Rep 5 · Final independent performance

Tell a 30–45 second story from school, college or work.

Key Point 3 · Talking about your education

Explain what your education gave you

subjectssoft skillstransferable skillscareer

1 · Warm-up

Which subject helped you most? Which skill from school or college do you still use now?

2 · Listen · Subject knowledge and soft skills

Riya: Was everything you studied useful for your career?

Dev: Not directly. I studied history, but I work in sales now.

Riya: So was history a waste of time?

Dev: No. It taught me to read carefully, compare evidence and explain ideas.

Riya: Those are transferable skills.

Dev: Exactly. My computer classes were more directly useful, but history helped in a different way.

3 · Notice the language

A strong answer links a subject to an effect: “History improved my ability to explain ideas.” Useful transferable skills include communication, problem-solving, teamwork, judgement and numeracy.

4 · Controlled task

Choose one subject. Say: subject → skill → example. Example: “Maths improved my accuracy because I had to check every step.”

Rep 1 · Easy supported attempt

Name one subject you liked and explain why.

5 · Correction / clarification

If your answer only names a subject, add the skill or effect it had on you.

Rep 2 · Less useful?

Name a subject that seemed less useful and explain fairly.

Rep 3 · Soft skill

Explain one transferable skill you developed through education.

Rep 4 · Debate response

“College should only teach job skills.” Respond.

Rep 5 · Final independent performance

Explain how your education prepared you for work or life.

Extra speaking gym

Debate practice

Record a 45–60 second answer. Give your position, two reasons and a short conclusion.

Do you think students should do part-time jobs while they are in school or college? Explain whether part-time work helps or harms their education, confidence and future careers.

Do you think soft skills are more important than specialist knowledge for a successful long-term career? Explain your view with reasons and examples.

Presentation practice

Make a presentation about your life so far. What stages in your life have you passed through to bring you where you are now? Include important stages from education, work and personal development where relevant.

Make a presentation about what colleges should prepare students for. What knowledge, skills, habits and real-world experiences do students need before they enter professional life?

Finish the unit

Repeat any task where your answer felt unclear or too dependent on the model.
