Background

Talk about your life story, past events and education in a clear, connected way. Each Key Point gives you five speaking reps: supported practice, a changed condition, and a final freer response.

Key Point 1 · Talking about events in life

Build a clear life line

backgroundlife eventsstudyworkfamily

Warm-up

Think of five important stages in your life. Which happened first, and which changed you most?

Listen · Natural conversation

Asha: I grew up in Jaipur, then I moved to Delhi for college.

Kabir: What did you study?

Asha: Economics. After college I joined a small consulting firm.

Kabir: And are you still there?

Asha: No. I left after two years and started a postgraduate programme.

Kabir: That sounds like a big change.

Asha: It was. I wanted to build stronger business skills.

Kabir: So what are you hoping to do next?

Work it out

Which answer gives a clearer background?

Language focus

Use common life-event combinations naturally: start school, leave school, go to college, join a company, get promoted, get married, have children, change career. Put events in a simple sequence so the listener can follow your background.

Rep 1 · Life line

Give three stages of your life in order.

Rep 2 · Add one change

Explain one important change in your study, work or personal life.

Rep 3 · Follow-up

Answer ‘Tell me about your background’ and add one detail the listener can ask about.

Rep 4 · Clarify

A listener asks, ‘What happened after college?’ Continue naturally.

Rep 5 · 30 seconds

Give a 30-second spoken life line.

Key Point 2 · Discussing past events

Tell a past story clearly

past simpleregular verbsirregular verbstime markers

Warm-up

Think about yesterday or last weekend. What happened, where were you, and who were you with?

Listen · Natural conversation

Nikhil: How was your weekend?

Meera: Busy. I visited my cousin on Saturday and helped her move house.

Nikhil: That sounds tiring.

Meera: It was. We carried boxes all morning, then we went out for lunch.

Nikhil: Did anything go wrong?

Meera: Yes. We lost one key, but we found it under a box later.

Nikhil: At least you found it.

Meera: Exactly. By evening we were exhausted, but everything was done.

Work it out

Which sentence is best for a finished past event?

Language focus

Use the past simple for finished events. Regular verbs often end in -ed; many common verbs are irregular: went, saw, met, took, found, bought, made. A good story needs sequence words such as first, then, after that, later, finally.

Rep 1 · Yesterday

Say three things you did yesterday.

Rep 2 · Sequence

Describe a short event using first, then and finally.

Rep 3 · Irregular verbs

Talk about a trip using went, saw and met.

Rep 4 · Problem and result

Describe a small problem that happened and how it ended.

Rep 5 · Personal story

Tell a 30-second story from school, college or work.

Key Point 3 · Talking about your education

Explain what your education gave you

school subjectssoft skillsuseful learningcareer

Warm-up

Which school or college subject helped you most? Was it useful because of the subject itself or because of a skill you developed?

Listen · Natural conversation

Riya: Was everything you studied useful for your career?

Dev: Not directly. I studied history, but I work in sales now.

Riya: So was history a waste of time?

Dev: No. It taught me to read carefully, compare evidence and explain ideas.

Riya: Those sound like transferable skills.

Dev: Exactly. My computer classes were more directly useful, but history helped in a different way.

Riya: What skill do you wish school had taught you?

Dev: Probably presenting ideas confidently.

Work it out

What makes an education answer stronger?

Language focus

Education gives both subject knowledge and soft or transferable skills. Instead of saying only ‘Maths was useful’, explain how: it improved your accuracy, problem-solving or confidence with numbers.

Rep 1 · Favourite subject

Name a subject you liked and explain why.

Rep 2 · Least useful?

Name a subject that seemed less useful and explain fairly.

Rep 3 · Soft skill

Explain one soft skill you developed through education.

Rep 4 · Challenge

Someone says, ‘College should only teach job skills.’ Respond.

Rep 5 · 30 seconds

Explain how your education prepared you for work or life.

Finish the unit

You have completed 15 speaking reps. Repeat any rep where your answer felt unclear, too short or too rehearsed.
