News

Discuss news topics, recent events and strong memories connected with events. Each Key Point gives you five speaking reps.

Key Point 1 · Discussing news stories

Classify and explain a story

headlinepoliticseconomycrimenatural disaster

Warm-up

What kinds of news do you follow most often, and which do you usually avoid?

Listen · Natural conversation

Karan: Did you see the main headline this morning?

Leena: The one about unemployment falling?

Karan: Yes. It's an economic story, but it could affect politics too.

Leena: True. I also saw a report about flooding in the north.

Karan: That's a natural-disaster story.

Leena: Exactly. And there was a crime report about a jewellery robbery.

Karan: A very mixed news day.

Leena: Yes. I prefer stories that explain what happened, not just dramatic headlines.

Work it out

Which word belongs most directly to an economic story?

Language focus

Useful news categories include politics, economy, crime, natural disasters, business and sport. Notice word families: politics → political → politician, economy → economic, crime → criminal, terror → terrorism → terrorist.

Rep 1 · Category

Choose a recent-style headline and say what category it belongs to.

Rep 2 · Vocabulary

Describe an economic, political or crime story using two topic words.

Rep 3 · Headline to story

Turn a short headline into two clear explanatory sentences.

Rep 4 · Bias check

A headline sounds dramatic. What more would you need to know?

Rep 5 · News briefing

Give a 30-second briefing on two different types of story.

Key Point 2 · Discussing recent events

Move from recent to specific detail

recentlylatelyjustin the last few days

Warm-up

What is the latest news from your work, study or family life?

Listen · Natural conversation

Amar: You look busy. What's been happening lately?

Naina: Quite a lot. I've just joined a new project.

Amar: Really? When did you start?

Naina: On Monday. I met the team that morning and spent the afternoon learning the system.

Amar: How has the first week been?

Naina: Good so far. I've been fairly busy recently, but the team has helped a lot.

Amar: Any surprises?

Naina: Yes. Yesterday they asked me to present an update next week.

Work it out

Which sentence correctly uses a finished past time?

Language focus

Use recently, lately, just and phrases such as in the last few days to introduce recent news. When you then give a finished time such as on Monday or yesterday, use the past simple.

Rep 1 · Just

Say one thing that has just happened.

Rep 2 · Recent to detail

Start with recently, then add one finished past detail.

Rep 3 · Ask more

A friend says, 'I've just started a new job.' Ask two natural follow-ups.

Rep 4 · Good or difficult news

Share one recent piece of good or difficult news and explain it.

Rep 5 · 30 seconds

Give a short 'latest from my side' update.

Key Point 3 · Talking about memories

Set the scene when something happened

remembermemorablewhenwhilepast continuous

Warm-up

What event do you remember so clearly that you can still say where you were and what you were doing?

Listen · Natural conversation

Priya: Do you remember when you got your final exam result?

Harsh: Very clearly. I was travelling home when the message arrived.

Priya: What were you doing exactly?

Harsh: I was standing on the metro platform, checking my phone.

Priya: Who did you tell first?

Harsh: My sister. I called her while I was waiting for the train.

Priya: Why is the memory so clear?

Harsh: Because I had been worrying about the result for weeks.

Work it out

Which sentence gives background action plus the event?

Language focus

Use the past continuous to set the background and the past simple for the event that happened: I was travelling when the phone rang. Use while naturally before a longer background action.

Rep 1 · Set the scene

Say where you were and what you were doing in one strong memory.

Rep 2 · When / while

Link the background action and the event with when or while.

Rep 3 · People and feeling

Add who was there and how you felt.

Rep 4 · Why remember?

Explain why the memory is still vivid.

Rep 5 · 30 seconds

Tell one vivid memory from start to reaction.

Finish the unit

You have completed 15 speaking reps. Repeat any rep where your answer felt unclear, too short or too rehearsed.
