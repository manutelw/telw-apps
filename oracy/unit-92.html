Achievements

Talk about experiences, progress towards goals and competition without losing the time frame. Each Key Point gives you five speaking reps.

Key Point 1 · Talking about experiences

Focus on what you have done

present perfecteverneverbeen togone to

Warm-up

What is one experience you have had that you still remember clearly?

Listen · Natural conversation

Ishita: Have you ever done anything really adventurous?

Rohan: I've tried river rafting, but I've never done a long trek.

Ishita: Where did you go rafting?

Rohan: I went in Rishikesh two years ago.

Ishita: So you've been to Rishikesh?

Rohan: Yes, twice. My brother has gone there this week, actually.

Ishita: Nice. Would you go rafting again?

Rohan: Definitely, but next time I'd like to try something harder.

Work it out

Which is correct with a finished time?

Language focus

Use the present perfect when the experience matters more than the exact past time: I've tried rafting. If you add a finished time, switch to the past simple: I went two years ago. Been to means visited and returned; gone to means the person is there or on the way.

Rep 1 · Have you ever?

Answer: Have you ever tried an unusual activity?

Rep 2 · Never

Say something you have never done but would like to do.

Rep 3 · Time switch

State an experience, then say when it happened.

Rep 4 · Been or gone

Explain the difference using people or places from your life.

Rep 5 · Experience story

Speak for 30 seconds about memorable experiences.

Key Point 2 · Discussing progress towards goals

Show what is done and what remains

alreadyyetstillso farforsince

Warm-up

Choose one current goal. What have you completed already, and what have you not done yet?

Listen · Natural conversation

Ananya: How's your certification going?

Vivek: Pretty well. I've finished four modules so far.

Ananya: Have you taken the practice test yet?

Vivek: Not yet. I still need to revise two topics.

Ananya: How long have you been preparing?

Vivek: For about six weeks. I've studied regularly since August.

Ananya: That's good progress.

Vivek: Yes. I've already booked the test, so now I have a deadline.

Work it out

Which sentence correctly reports unfinished progress?

Language focus

Already shows something is completed earlier than expected. Yet is common in questions and negatives. Still shows something continues or remains. So far measures progress up to now. Use for with a period and since with a starting point.

Rep 1 · So far

State your progress on one current goal.

Rep 2 · Already / yet

Say one thing already done and one thing not done yet.

Rep 3 · For / since

Say how long you have worked on the goal.

Rep 4 · Pressure

Someone says, 'You're behind schedule.' Explain your actual progress.

Rep 5 · Progress update

Give a 30-second progress report.

Key Point 3 · Talking about competition

Describe what people are competing for

competecompetitorbeatwincandidate

Warm-up

Where do you see competition outside sport—jobs, business, awards, college admissions, or something else?

Listen · Natural conversation

Sahil: People usually think competition means sport.

Maya: Yes, but companies compete for contracts and candidates compete for jobs too.

Sahil: True. Is competition always good?

Maya: Not always, but it can push people to improve.

Sahil: What matters more: beating others or improving yourself?

Maya: For me, improving yourself. Winning matters, but progress matters too.

Sahil: And in a team?

Maya: Then you compete externally but cooperate internally.

Work it out

Which phrase is natural?

Language focus

Use compete with/against a person or organisation, compete for a prize, role or contract, beat an opponent, and win a match, award or contract. A candidate competes for selection; a competitor competes in a market, contest or sport.

Rep 1 · Example

Give one example of competition outside sport.

Rep 2 · Vocabulary

Use compete for, win and competitor in connected sentences.

Rep 3 · Opinion

Is competition good for performance? Give a reason.

Rep 4 · Counterpoint

Someone says, 'Competition always damages teamwork.' Respond.

Rep 5 · 30 seconds

Explain healthy competition in work or study.

Finish the unit

You have completed 15 speaking reps. Repeat any rep where your answer felt unclear, too short or too rehearsed.
