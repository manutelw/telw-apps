Key Point 1 · Discussing travel procedures

Explain the journey step by step

check-insecuritypassport controlgateplatformboarding pass

Warm-up

Imagine a friend is flying alone for the first time. What would you tell them to do at the airport?

Listen · Natural conversation

Farah: I've never flown alone. What do I do first?

Aditya: Check the departures screen, then go to the check-in desk if you need to check luggage.

Farah: What happens after check-in?

Aditya: You go through security. Your bag is scanned and you walk through a detector.

Farah: Then passport control?

Aditya: For an international flight, yes. After that, find your gate and wait for boarding.

Farah: And the boarding pass shows my seat?

Aditya: Exactly—usually the gate, seat number and boarding details.

Work it out

Where do you normally go to board the correct flight?

Language focus

A typical flight sequence is departures → check-in → security → passport control → gate → boarding. Train language includes platform, track, coach; flight language includes boarding pass, gate, aisle seat, window seat.

Key Point 2 · Talking about frequency

Say how often—not just yes or no

usuallyoftensometimesoccasionallyrarelynever

Warm-up

How often do you use different modes of transport in a normal week?

Listen · Natural conversation

Sameer: How do you normally get to work?

Aditi: I usually take the metro. I drive occasionally if I have a late meeting.

Sameer: Do you ever take the bus?

Aditi: Rarely. The metro is quicker for my route.

Sameer: What about longer trips?

Aditi: I often take the train if the journey is under five hours.

Sameer: And flying?

Aditi: Only sometimes. I never fly for a short journey if a good train is available.

Work it out

Which sentence has the frequency adverb in the usual position?

Language focus

A useful scale is always → usually/normally → often/frequently → sometimes/occasionally → rarely/seldom → never. Put these adverbs before most main verbs: I usually drive, but after be: I am usually early.

Rep 1 · Daily transport

Say how often you use two kinds of transport.

Rep 2 · Frequency + reason

Say how often you use one mode and explain why.

Rep 3 · Frequent vs rare

Contrast something you do often with something you rarely do.

Rep 4 · Correct the idea

Someone says, 'You always drive.' Correct them naturally.

Rep 5 · Weekly habits

Give a 30-second picture of your travel habits.

Key Point 3 · Talking about travel problems

Explain the problem and the next move

missdelaycancelledbreak downget lostrefund

Warm-up

What travel problem worries you most: traffic, a delay, getting lost, missing a connection, or something else?

Listen · Natural conversation

Traveller: My first train was delayed, so I missed my connection.

Agent: Let me check your ticket. This fare was only valid on the train you missed.

Traveller: Does that mean I have to buy another ticket?

Agent: I'm afraid so. The next service has a standard fare.

Traveller: Can I pay just the difference?

Agent: Not with this ticket. It was a reduced advance fare.

Traveller: Is there any refund on the original ticket?

Agent: No, but I can show you the cheapest option for the next train.

Work it out

What does 'miss your connection' mean?

Language focus

Useful problem chunks include miss a flight/connection, run out of fuel, break down, get stuck in traffic, get lost, be delayed, be cancelled. A clear problem report follows problem → consequence → request or next action.

Rep 1 · State the problem

Say one travel problem clearly in one sentence.

Rep 2 · Consequence

Add what happened because of the problem.

Rep 3 · Request

Ask an official for a practical solution.

Rep 4 · Pressure

Your flight is cancelled and the queue is long. Explain what you need.

Rep 5 · Problem story

Tell a 30-second travel problem: problem, action, result.
