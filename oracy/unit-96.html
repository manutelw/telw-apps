Planning

Make arrangements, describe fixed plans and timetables, and distinguish earlier plans from decisions made now. Each Key Point gives you five speaking reps.

Key Point 1 · Making arrangements

Link actions and make offers

whenas soon asuntilshall II'll

Warm-up

Think of something you are organising. What has to happen first, and what can only happen after that?

Listen · Natural conversation

Nat: We need to confirm the workshop dates.

Aman: I'll call the training team when I get to the office.

Nat: Great. Shall I check the hotel options?

Aman: Yes, please. And I'll send you the budget as soon as I get an answer from finance.

Nat: Should I book anything now?

Aman: Not yet. Don't book the rooms until we confirm the dates.

Nat: Fine. I'll wait for your message.

Aman: Perfect. Then we can finalise everything.

Work it out

Which sentence is correct?

Language focus

Use when, as soon as and until to link future actions, but normally do not use will inside the time clause: I'll call when I arrive. Use Shall I...? to offer help and I'll... for a decision or commitment.

Rep 1 · When

Make one future arrangement using when.

Rep 2 · As soon as

Say what you will do immediately after something happens.

Rep 3 · Until

Say what you will not do before another action is complete.

Rep 4 · Offer

Your partner is organising a meeting. Offer to help with one task.

Rep 5 · Arrangement chain

Give a 30-second sequence using when, as soon as and until.

Key Point 2 · Describing arrangements

Separate personal arrangements from timetables

present continuouspresent simplemeetingflightschedule

Warm-up

What is one personal arrangement you have this week? What fixed timetable or schedule is connected to it?

Listen · Natural conversation

Ritu: What are you doing on Thursday?

Omar: I'm meeting a client at eleven.

Ritu: Are you travelling that day?

Omar: Yes. My train leaves at seven thirty, so I'm leaving home at six.

Ritu: When does the conference start?

Omar: It starts at two. I'm speaking in the second session.

Ritu: And when are you coming back?

Omar: I'm returning on Friday evening. The train arrives at nine.

Work it out

Which sentence describes a fixed timetable?

Language focus

Use the present continuous for personal future arrangements: I'm meeting a client. Use the present simple for fixed timetables and schedules: The train leaves at 7:30.

Key Point 3 · Discussing plans and decisions

Show when the decision was made

willgoing todecision nowearlier plan

Warm-up

What have you already decided to do later today? What decision might you make only when a situation appears?

Listen · Natural conversation

Pooja: We still need someone to call the venue.

Arjun: I'll do it.

Pooja: Thanks. What about the presentation?

Arjun: I'm going to finish it tonight. I decided that this morning.

Pooja: And the handouts?

Arjun: Oh, I forgot about those. I'll print them after lunch.

Pooja: Good. I'm going to ask Mira to check the final list; we've already agreed on that.

Arjun: Great. Then we're nearly sorted.

Work it out

Which sentence most clearly shows a decision made now?

Language focus

Use will naturally for a decision made at the moment of speaking: I'll do it. Use going to for an intention or decision made earlier: I'm going to finish it tonight; I decided this morning.
