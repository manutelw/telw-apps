TELW Levels

What you can do in English at each level, from A1 to C2+, with real examples from work and daily life.

Find my level

The scale

Your TELW Level comes from two parts: a grammar and vocabulary test, and a speaking test. TELW has 10 levels. Each one is shown with its CEFR equivalent (the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages). TELW levels are finer than CEFR levels, so two TELW levels can share one CEFR level. Tap a level to see what it means.

A1CEFR A1

Speaking

You can say who you are, where you live and what you do, using words and phrases you have learned.

For example, “My name is Ravi. I live in Noida. I work in a bank.”

Understanding

You can follow very slow, clear speech about yourself and your family.

For example, you understand “What is your phone number?” and “Where do you work?” when they are asked slowly.

At work

You can greet people, ask for help and answer simple questions, if the other person speaks slowly.

For example, “Good morning. Where is the meeting room?” and “Yes, I am from the sales team.”

A2CEFR A2

Speaking

You can talk about your daily routine, your job or studies, and your family in short, simple sentences.

For example, speak for about 30 seconds on what you do on a working day, with pauses between sentences.

Understanding

You can follow short, clear announcements and instructions.

For example, a platform change announced at a metro station, or “Please send me the file before lunch.”

At work

You can handle simple, routine exchanges, such as booking a room, asking the way or explaining a small problem.

For example, telling the office admin that your laptop charger is not working.

B1CEFR B1

Speaking

You can talk about your experiences, plans and opinions on familiar topics and give simple reasons, though you pause and make mistakes.

For example, say what you think of your city’s traffic and give one or two reasons, in about a minute, stopping often to find words.

Understanding

You can follow the main points of clear speech on everyday and work topics.

For example, after a short team briefing you can say what was decided, though you miss some details.

At work

You can take part in a routine team meeting on a familiar topic and say what you did and what you need.

For example, in a daily stand-up: “Yesterday I finished the report. Today I need the sales figures from Priya.”

B1+CEFR B1

Speaking

You can link your ideas with words like because, so and but, and keep going for a minute or two without losing your point.

For example, describe a project you worked on for 1–2 minutes: what it was, what went wrong, and what you did about it.

Understanding

You can follow a short talk or discussion on a familiar subject if people speak clearly, and catch most of the detail.

For example, a 5-minute product demo by a colleague, after which you can list the main features.

At work

You can describe a process, give simple instructions, and handle most everyday situations on the phone or in person.

For example, reporting a problem to IT, changing a booking, or explaining a delay to a customer without needing help.

B2CEFR B1+

Speaking

You can give and explain your opinion, compare options, and say which one you prefer and why.

For example, compare two vendors for 2 minutes on cost, quality and delivery, and recommend one.

Understanding

You can follow most of a meeting on work topics, including some detail.

For example, a 30-minute review meeting, after which you can tell a colleague the main points and who has to do what.

At work

You can handle an interview or a client conversation on familiar ground, and ask questions to check you have understood.

For example, “Just to confirm, you need the samples by the 10th, not the 15th?”

B2+CEFR B1+

Speaking

You can support your views with examples, with only occasional pauses to find words.

For example, answer “Why should we hire you?” for 2 minutes with two real examples from your work, without long silences.

Understanding

You can follow discussions at normal speed when the topic is familiar.

For example, a client call where people speak at normal speed and sometimes talk over each other.

At work

You can give your team an update, answer follow-up questions, and handle a complaint politely and firmly.

For example, “I understand you’re upset. We can’t refund the full amount, but we can replace the product this week.”

C1CEFR B2

Speaking

You can speak clearly and in detail on a wide range of subjects, and explain the pros and cons of different options.

For example, speak for 3 minutes on whether your company should allow work from home, covering both sides.

Understanding

You can follow long talks and detailed arguments on familiar topics, and most news and discussion programmes.

For example, a 45-minute conference talk in your field, or a business news debate on TV.

At work

You can take an active part in meetings, negotiations and interviews with fluent speakers, and both sides can talk comfortably.

For example, negotiating a price with a supplier, where neither side has to slow down or repeat.

C1+CEFR B2

Speaking

You speak smoothly and with good control. You can change your tone for a senior manager, a client or a colleague.

For example, speak at normal speed for 3–4 minutes with few pauses, and say “no” to a senior politely without sounding rude.

Understanding

You can follow fast discussions, and pick up opinions and attitudes that are hinted at rather than said.

For example, noticing that “Let’s revisit this next quarter” from a client really means “not now”.

At work

You can lead a discussion, manage disagreement, and steer a meeting towards a decision.

For example, chairing a 30-minute meeting where two teams disagree, and ending it with an agreed next step.

C2CEFR B2+

Speaking

You can argue a case fluently and accurately. Your mistakes are rare and do not cause confusion.

For example, defending a budget request for 5 minutes with no more than one or two small errors that nobody notices.

Understanding

You can follow demanding talks and discussions in your field, including technical discussion.

For example, a technical design review, or a discussion with your company’s lawyers about a contract.

At work

You can present to senior people and handle tough questions without losing your thread.

For example, a 10-minute presentation to the leadership team, then 10 minutes of challenging questions.

C2+CEFR B2+

Speaking

You speak fluently, accurately and persuasively, and choose your words for the effect you want.

For example, opening a pitch with one line that makes the client lean in.

Understanding

You understand nearly everything in your field, including humour, hints and what is left unsaid.

For example, noticing that a board member’s joke is really a warning about the budget.

At work

You can represent your organisation when the stakes are high, such as in board meetings, pitches and final interviews.

For example, presenting your company’s case to an investor, or handling a final-round panel interview.

The descriptions on this page are TELW’s own. The CEFR equivalents follow the Council of Europe’s CEFR levels. Read about the CEFR.
