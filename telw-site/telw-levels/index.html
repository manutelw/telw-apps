TELW Levels

What you can do in English at each level, from A1 to C2B, when you listen and speak.

The scale

TELW has 11 levels, from A1 to C2B. Each level tells you what you can do when you listen and speak, in three parts: listening, spoken interaction (talking with others) and spoken production (speaking on your own). Each level builds on the one below it. Tap a level to see what it means.

A1

Listening

I can understand simple directions, such as “Turn left at the end of the road.” I can understand a short, simple phone message, such as “We’re arriving tomorrow at 4.30.”

Spoken interaction

I can ask simple questions about facts, such as “Where is the dining room?”, and understand short answers in simple language. I can take part in a basic conversation on a predictable topic, such as my home, family or school.

Spoken production

I can make a simple complaint, such as “The water is cold.” I can pass on a simple routine message, such as “Friday meeting, 10 a.m.”

A2A

Listening

I can understand simple questions and instructions, such as “Take this to the pharmacy.” I can understand a simple phone message.

Spoken interaction

I can ask for what I need in a shop or office, if it is easy to understand. I can order a meal and ask simple questions about the menu. I can answer routine questions, such as “How long are you staying?”

Spoken production

I can say what I like and don’t like, in simple words. I can state a simple need in my job, such as “I want to order 25.”

A2B

Listening

I can understand the general meaning of a simple talk if it is backed up by pictures. I can follow the outline of simple information on a guided tour. I can understand a phone message and confirm the details.

Spoken interaction

I can take part in small talk and routine, predictable conversations. I can give and understand short, simple directions. I can ask for and understand information at a travel or tourist office.

Spoken production

I can give a simple opinion, such as “This is better because…” I can give simple explanations about places I know. I can make a call and pass on a simple, prepared message.

B1A

Listening

I can follow a simple talk or demonstration on a topic in my own field. I can understand public announcements and instructions. I can follow the general outline of a guided tour of a familiar kind of place.

Spoken interaction

I can greet a visitor and chat for a short time about their journey or hotel. I can book a room or an appointment over the phone. I can have a simple phone conversation with someone I know on a predictable topic.

Spoken production

I can explain what is wrong in plain terms at a chemist’s or doctor’s. I can give my opinion in simple words, such as “I don’t agree”, if the question is put clearly and simply.

B1B

Listening

I can follow the story of a film when the action shows what is happening. I can pick out the main topic of a TV news item when there are strong pictures.

Spoken interaction

I can deal with most routine situations when I travel, such as buying tickets or checking in. I can ask questions to find out facts, such as what is wrong with a machine, and understand simple replies. I can join a casual or semi-formal conversation for a short time.

Spoken production

I can complain about simple matters, such as the service or the bill. I can state routine needs in my own area of work.

B2A

Listening

I can understand the general meaning of a lecture or talk on a familiar topic, in clear, simple language. I can understand when someone explains the difference between two products. I can take a more complex phone message if the caller speaks clearly and patiently.

Spoken interaction

I can keep up a casual conversation for a fair length of time on mainly familiar topics. I can deal with predictable requests from a visitor. I can take a routine order, such as quantity and delivery date.

Spoken production

I can give simple advice to a client in my own area of work, such as “This model gives better copies, but it costs more.” I can give a short, simple talk on a familiar topic.

B2B

Listening

I can understand most explanations I am likely to hear in a shop, hotel or restaurant. I can understand advice given in everyday language.

Spoken interaction

I can take a limited part in a class or meeting discussion, if it is run patiently and in simple language. I can ask for things to be explained again. I can check that a place or service meets all my needs.

Spoken production

I can give opinions on cultural and abstract topics in a limited way. I can answer routine questions and give simple explanations.

C1A

Listening

I can understand most of what is said on a guided tour. I can follow a presentation or demonstration of a product. I can understand the main points of an agreement, such as deposits and bills, when it is explained to me.

Spoken interaction

I can keep up a conversation on a fairly wide range of topics, such as my experiences, my work and the news. I can take or leave routine phone messages and ask for anything unclear to be explained. I can ask for reasons and clarification during a talk.

Spoken production

I can give a clear, prepared presentation on a familiar topic, such as a product, and answer most factual questions about it. I can describe what happened in an incident, such as a theft or a breakdown.

C1B

Listening

I can understand most of what is said in a TV or radio programme, or in a film. I can take and pass on most of the messages that come up in a normal working day.

Spoken interaction

I can have a casual phone conversation with someone I know on a variety of topics. I can complain effectively about most problems that are likely to come up. I can bargain for what I want and reach an agreement.

Spoken production

I can give my opinion and back it up. I can tell the main points from side points and asides. I can state my needs clearly and in detail.

C2A

Listening

I can understand most of what takes place in a meeting on practical matters. I can follow a talk well enough to ask useful questions about it.

Spoken interaction

I can make a limited contribution to meetings on practical matters, such as solving a problem, when the language is fairly simple. I can handle most of what comes up when I host people or am their guest. I can check that I have understood all instructions.

Spoken production

I can put my point across persuasively, for example about a product I know well. I can present arguments, to a limited extent.

C2B

Listening

I can understand most of what takes place around me, and most of the messages and information in a normal working day.

Spoken interaction

I can express my opinions on abstract and cultural topics, and defend them. I can keep up a conversation on a fairly wide range of topics, with ease.

Spoken production

I can argue my case and put my point across persuasively about a familiar product or situation.
