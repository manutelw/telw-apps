How feedback works

One question, one answer, and what a trainer actually says about it. Interview question: “Tell me about yourself.”

  1. 1

    The usual answer

    “Myself Ajay. I am from Lucknow. I completed my B.Com from Delhi University in 2025. My father is a businessman and my mother is a homemaker. I am hardworking, punctual and a quick learner. My hobbies are reading books and listening to music. That’s all about me.”
  2. 2

    The trainer’s feedback

    • This is a biodata, not an answer. Hometown, parents and hobbies tell the interviewer nothing about why you suit the job. Open with what you do and what you are good at.
    • Adjectives prove nothing. Every candidate is “hardworking, punctual and a quick learner”. Drop the list and give one example that shows it.
    • The ending closes the door. “That’s all about me” invites a random next question. End by linking yourself to the role, so the next question goes where you want it.
    • One small point: “Myself Ajay” becomes “I’m Ajay”. Interviewers notice it.
  3. 3

    The retry

    “I’m Ajay, a commerce graduate from Delhi University. Over the past year I have focused on accounts. During my internship at a CA firm, I reconciled bank statements for twelve small clients and helped cut the monthly closing time from five days to three. I enjoy work where accuracy matters. That’s why this analyst role interests me, and I’d like to talk about how I could support your reporting team.”

Nothing about Ajay changed. Only what he chose to say.

How the training works

A capacity-building approach, not a coaching class. Every learner follows the same four stages.

  1. Diagnose

    A one-to-one fluency diagnostic identifies your level and your specific blockers.

  2. Place

    You start at the right level, with a bridge course if the skill programme needs one.

  3. Train

    Small-group or one-to-one sessions focused on doing the skill, not hearing about it.

  4. Practise and measure

    Scored practice and feedback between sessions, with reports on progress.

Frequently asked questions

What happens in the fluency diagnostic?

A one-to-one conversation. You get your current level with spoken feedback, the two or three habits holding you back, a sample of how the training works, and an improvement plan based on your goal.

Do I need good English before joining a skills programme?

Skills programmes such as presentation, interview and email writing need a working level of English. If the diagnostic shows a gap, you start with a General English bridge course first.

Is training available online?

Yes. Classroom training runs in New Delhi, live sessions run online, and placement practice is available anytime on ClarionPrep.

Do you train groups for colleges and companies?

Yes. Institutional programmes cover B-school placement readiness and workplace communication for teams, with diagnostics and progress reporting for the people who commission the training.

Where do I practise group discussions and interviews?

Apart from our online and offline classes, you can practise on ClarionPrep.com, which offers GD practice, PI practice, an AI mock interview, a written communication test and the Placement Readiness Pass.
